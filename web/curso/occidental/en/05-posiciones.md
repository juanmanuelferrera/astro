# Module 5 — Positions

**Aim:** to be able to read one line of a chart — "Sun in Libra at 24° in the 2nd house" — and
say what each of the four pieces contributes.

## The idea

Every position is a sentence in three parts, always in the same order:

| Element | Question it answers | Grammatical role |
|---|---|---|
| **Planet** | Which function? | the subject |
| **Sign** | In what manner? | the adverb |
| **House** | On what ground? | the place |
| **Degree** | With what precision? | it sharpens, and it decides aspects |

"The Sun in Libra in the 2nd house" translates, raw, as: *your vitality and your identity
express themselves seeking balance and agreement, on the ground of resources and what you own.*

That is a clumsy sentence, and **it has to be**. The literal translation comes first; decent
English comes afterwards. Whoever skips the clumsy step starts guessing.

## The planets: which function

- **Sun** — identity, vitality, purpose, the father
- **Moon** — reaction, need, habit, the mother
- **Mercury** — thinking, speaking, moving, connecting
- **Venus** — valuing, attracting, harmonising, enjoying
- **Mars** — wanting, pushing, separating, defending
- **Jupiter** — expanding, trusting, giving meaning
- **Saturn** — limiting, structuring, lasting, demanding
- **Uranus** — breaking, waking, setting free
- **Neptune** — dissolving, imagining, confusing, pitying
- **Pluto** — emptying, forcing, regenerating

## The signs: in what manner

Before the twelve, two grids that are worth more:

**Elements.** Fire (burns, starts) · Earth (holds, makes concrete) · Air (communicates,
abstracts) · Water (feels, retains).

**Modalities.** Cardinal (sets off) · Fixed (sustains) · Mutable (adapts).

Every sign is a crossing of the two. Scorpio is fixed water: it feels and does not let go.
Gemini is mutable air: it connects and changes. With these two grids the twelve can be deduced
without memorising lists.

## The degree, which is not decoration

The degree does three jobs:
1. It decides the **aspects** and how exact they are (module 7)
2. It places the planet near or far from a **cusp** — a planet at 29° of a house already has
   one foot in the next
3. It marks the **boundary positions**: the last and first degrees of a sign have a character of
   their own, of ending and of beginning

## Questions

1. Translate literally, without dressing it up: **Mars in Pisces in the 6th house**.
2. What changes if that same Mars is in the 10th house?
3. And if it stays in the 6th but in Aries?
4. Why does the planet carry more weight than the sign?
   *(The planet is the function; the sign only modulates it. Without a function there is nothing
   to modulate.)*

## Exercise

Have them translate **the ten planets of their chart**, one by one, in clumsy literal sentences.
No interpreting yet, no softening, nothing joined up.

The result will be an ugly, repetitive text. Good: that is raw material, and in module 10 it
turns into prose.

## Closing question

**If two people have the Moon in Cancer, one in the 4th house and one in the 10th, what do they
share and where do they differ?**

## Common mistakes

- Starting with the sign. The subject of the sentence is the planet
- Skipping the clumsy translation and going straight to the pretty sentence
- Treating the degree as an ornament
- Memorising the twelve signs as lists of adjectives instead of deducing them from element and
  modality
