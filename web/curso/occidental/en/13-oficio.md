# Module 13 — The craft, ethics and limits

**Aim:** to know what is done with this and what is not. It is the most important module and the
one almost nobody teaches.

## Why it comes here

Module 10 produced a conclusion: **the one who squares up the picture is the reader.** The chart
does not interpret by itself. And whoever decides what to say is answerable for what is said.

A reading reaches somebody at a vulnerable moment, and what they are told can stay with them for
years. That imposes obligations.

## Margaret Hone's rules

Written in 1953 and still the best summary there is:

**Think of the client before yourself.** Not of how brilliant you can look.

**Ask yourself why they have come and what they really need.** It is rarely what they say at the
start.

**Ask for the history.** If they hide their situation, explain that they would not go to a doctor
demanding a diagnosis while concealing the symptoms.

**And if they insist on hiding it**, in her words: it is better to explain that you are an
astrological practitioner and not a fortune-teller, and to say that you would rather not take
their case any further.

**Write every reading whole for every person.** No prefabricated paragraphs cut and pasted.

**No jargon.** The symbols go in the margin of your copy, not in what the other person reads.

## What is never said

- **Dates of death.** Neither their own nor a relative's. Under no amount of insistence.
- **Medical diagnoses.** You are not a doctor and the chart does not diagnose.
- **Predictions of serious illness or of infertility.**
- **Verdicts on other people** who are not present and have asked for nothing.
- **Nothing in a tone of fate.** Everything said must leave room for action.

And a practical rule: **if something cannot be said in a way that is useful, it is not said.** A
truth that only produces fear is not information, it is harm.

## The real limits

**The chart does not state facts.** It states tendencies, grounds and periods. It does not say
whether you will marry: it says how you form ties.

**No two readings are alike.** Twelve trained astrologers produce twelve different texts on the
same chart, alike underneath and different in form — like twelve painters of the same landscape.
Anyone who tells you their reading is *the* reading is lying.

**Astrology replaces nothing.** Not a doctor, not a lawyer, not a therapist.

**Working backwards proves nothing.** Knowing the biography, anyone will find factors that fit —
with any chart. The only honest test is to deduce without knowing, and then check.

## How you calibrate yourself

- **Write your readings down before you check them.** Written and dated.
- **Count the misses as carefully as the hits.** Whoever only remembers the hits learns nothing.
- **Practise blind.** Charts of people you do not know, deducing first.
- **Keep the readings.** Five years on, rereading them is the best training there is.

## Questions

1. A client asks when their mother will die. What do you do?
2. Why is "this fits me" no proof of anything?
3. You see something hard in a child's chart and you are talking to the parents. How do you put
   it?
4. How do you know whether you are improving?

## Exercise

Have them reread the reading they wrote in module 10 and mark:
- which sentences are **data** and which are **inference**
- which sentences they would have written the same way about a stranger
- which sentences they would not say out loud to the person, and why

That third point is usually the most revealing of the course.

## Closing question

**If the reader is the one who squares up the picture, whose responsibility is what the chart
says?**

## Common mistakes

- Confusing frankness with cruelty
- Using the chart to confirm what you already think of somebody
- Accepting consultations about absent third parties
- Believing that remembered hits amount to a track record
