# Module 9 — Combining and counteracting

**Aim:** the step up in the course. Until now they have read loose pieces. Here they learn to
hold pieces that contradict each other — which is what makes a chart real.

## The idea to accept first

**No aspect is read alone.** Every planet arrives loaded with all of its own at once, and those
aspects almost never say the same thing.

Saturn squares Venus: loving comes hard, there is reserve, fear of rejection. But Jupiter trines
that same Venus: there is generosity, trust, warmth.

The beginner picks one of the two and throws the other away. **That is the central error of all
badly done astrology.**

The right answer is not to choose. It is to understand that the person **has both**, and that
this is exactly what has to be described: someone warm who pulls back, generous and mistrustful
at once, who gives a great deal and finds it hard to ask.

> **A contradiction in the chart is not a failure of reading. It is a portrait.**

Real people are made of contradictions. A chart that said one thing cleanly would describe
somebody who does not exist.

## How the pieces are weighed

Holding both sides does not mean they weigh the same. There are criteria, and in this order:

**1. Exactness.** The first and the most reliable. A square at 1° rules over a trine at 7°. If
Saturn squares Venus at 0.5° and Jupiter trines it at 6°, the reserve is the dominant trait and
the warmth is the shading — not the other way round.

**2. Applying or separating.** What is forming weighs more than what has already passed.

**3. The dignity of those involved.** A Saturn in domicile afflicts with authority and produces
structure. A Saturn in fall afflicts without giving anything back. The same aspect, two stories.

**4. The rulers.** Here is the step up. "Saturn squares Venus" is a textbook sentence. **"The
ruler of the 2nd squares the ruler of the 7th"** says that money gets complicated *through*
relationships. Rulers turn loose aspects into arguments with a subject and a ground.

**5. The general shape of the chart.** All of this happens inside a climate: hemispheres, a
stellium, a T-square, empty houses. The whole modifies each part.

## The master rule

When two readings fight and the criteria do not settle it, count:

> **If three independent significators say the same thing, it is real.
> If only one says it, it is a murmur.**

Genuinely independent. If the Sun, the ruler of the Ascendant and the angular planet are the
same planet, that is **one** testimony said three times, not three testimonies.

This rule does two jobs: it settles contradictions and — more importantly — **it prevents
invention**. If something appears only once, it is mentioned with reservations or not at all.

## The procedure

1. Write down **every** clumsy sentence, including the ones that contradict each other. Discard
   nothing yet.
2. Group them by theme: character, mind, work, ties, family, health.
3. Inside each group, **mark the contradictions out loud**. Do not hide them.
4. Weigh them with the five criteria. Decide what is a dominant trait and what is shading.
5. Count convergences. Whatever only one factor says goes in with reservations.
6. Write **one sentence that contains both halves**. That is the craft.

## From contradiction to sentence

| Raw | Wrong (picks a side) | Right (holds both) |
|---|---|---|
| Mars in 10 · Saturn squares MC | "He will have a great career" | "He pushes hard towards public life and keeps hitting ceilings; he arrives, but late and under his own steam" |
| Moon in 9 · Saturn in 4 | "A free spirit, she will travel" | "She needs horizon and drags a heavy root; she leaves and comes back" |
| Venus in Pisces · Venus squares Uranus | "Romantic and devoted" | "She gives herself completely and needs a way out; she loves without reserve and suffocates if shut in" |

Note the structure: **two halves joined by "and", not by "but".** The "but" subtracts; the "and"
describes.

## Questions

1. Mercury trine Jupiter with 5° of orb, and Mercury square Saturn with 1°. How does that person
   think? Write **one** sentence.
2. Why might "the Sun, the ruler of the Ascendant and the angular planet" not be three
   testimonies?
3. An isolated factor says there will be health trouble and nothing else confirms it. What do you
   do?
4. What is the difference between joining two traits with "but" and joining them with "and"?

## Exercise

Have them find **the strongest contradiction** in their chart: two factors saying opposite things
about the same matter. Have them weigh it with the five criteria and write a single sentence
that contains both.

Then have them find a theme where **three factors converge**. That is a safe trait, and they
know why it is one.

## Closing question

**Why would a chart with no contradictions be a badly read chart?**

## Common mistakes

- Keeping the aspect you like best and saying nothing about the other
- Adding factors up instead of weighing them
- Counting as independent three testimonies that are the same planet by three routes
- Softening the contradictions until what is left is a portrait of nobody
