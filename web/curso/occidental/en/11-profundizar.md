# Module 11 — Going deeper: from the general to the cause

**Aim:** to know what to do when a reading comes out blurred. Not to embellish: to calculate.

Before giving this module, read `motor-profundizacion.md`. That is the procedure; this is the
lesson.

## The idea

Module 10 produces a complete reading. And a complete reading is full of sentences like "there
is tension with the father", "money is an issue" or "he finds it hard to finish things".

They are true and they are useless. They do not say **what** exactly, nor **why**, nor **what to
do**.

Almost everybody stops there and fills the gap with adjectives. The craft consists of the
opposite: **going back to the calculation**.

## What has to be understood

A chart is not used up by one reading. It is a source that answers narrower and narrower
questions, as long as you know which instrument to apply.

> **Every more precise question demands one more calculation, not one more word.**

And causes nest. There is almost never only one: there is one inside another. "Trouble with the
father" turns out to be an absence; the absence turns out to come from the father's own history;
and that in turn is held up by something earlier. Going down through those layers is the work.

## The loop

1. **General finding** — state it exactly as it came out
2. **Where does it come from?** — list the factors. Only one? It is a murmur; say so and stop
3. **Which calculation sharpens it?** — go up one rung of the scale
4. **A narrower finding** — rewrite the sentence
5. **Still blurred?** — back to step 2

## The scale

Ruler of the house → chain of dispositors → aspects to the ruler by exactness → derived house →
dignity and accidental state → prediction.

One rung at a time. Skipping three produces readings that sound deep and are not.

## Where you stop

This is half the module, and the half almost nobody teaches.

- When **three independent instruments converge**
- When **the next calculation would not change the advice**
- When **you stop calculating and start narrating** — the moment a layer comes out of your head
  rather than out of a position, it is over

And it has to be said: **"this is as far as the chart goes"** is a complete professional answer.
Whoever cannot say it ends up inventing, because the pressure to have an answer is enormous.

## The precision trap

The further down you go, the more inference sounds like data. A calculation to four decimal
places does not make the interpretation you rest on it true.

At every layer, separate them and say so:
- **Data:** positions, aspects, orbs. Astronomy. Verifiable.
- **Inference:** what it means. Arguable, and presented as such.

## Questions

1. "Money trouble" comes out. Which is the first instrument, and why?
   *(The ruler of the 2nd and where it sits: it connects the matter with the ground it comes
   from.)*
2. You have gone down five layers and each one sounds better than the last. A good sign?
   *(Not necessarily. If the last ones do not come from concrete positions, you are narrating.)*
3. What is the difference between going deeper and embellishing?
4. When is it right to say you do not know?

## Exercise

Have them take **the blurriest sentence** from the reading they wrote in module 10 and apply the
loop to it. Have them document every layer: which instrument they used, what they found, how the
sentence ended up.

And have them note **where they stopped and why**. That is worth as much as the result.

## Closing question

**Why is the stopping rule as important as the loop?**

## Common mistakes

- Confusing more layers with more truth
- Going down a ladder that no longer touches real positions
- Never saying "I do not know"
- Presenting the inference of layer five with the same confidence as the data of layer zero
