# The short way — the whole system in five blocks

**Use:** this is given **in one sitting**, in about 17 minutes. Five blocks chained together, no
stopping between them, no exercises, no asking "shall we go on?".

Aim: that by the end they understand **what a chart is, how it is built and how it is read**. Not
that they can do it.

**Give the content there is, and stop.** Do not pad to make it longer: if a block is short, that
is because it is complete. You may reword it so it sounds spoken, not to add material. Whatever
is missing is in the long way.

---

## Block 1 — What a chart is (≈2 min)

An ephemeris says where the planets are today, and that is the same for everybody. What turns
"today's sky" into "the sky **from here**" is two circles:

- **The horizon** of the birthplace. It cuts the ecliptic in the east at the **Ascendant** and in
  the west at the **Descendant**. It separates what was visible from what was hidden.
- **The meridian**, which passes overhead. It cuts at the **Midheaven** (what was culminating)
  and the **Imum Coeli** (under the feet).

Those four points are the **angles**. The two circles cross at a right angle and cut the chart
into four quadrants.

The key to all of it: the planets move slowly — the Sun takes a month to change sign — but **the
horizon moves one degree every four minutes**. That is why the hour and the place matter so much:
the planets you share with thousands of people born that day; the horizon is the only thing that
is yours.

And out of that comes the sentence that orders the whole system: **every house system agrees on
the four angles; they differ only in how to subdivide between them.**

---

## Block 2 — How it is put up (≈4 min)

Three steps.

**The hour.** The most fragile piece of data, and where nearly all the errors are: daylight
saving, which changes by country and by year, and the time zone, which is a political decision
and not an astronomical one. Half an hour of error is about seven degrees of Ascendant.

And before converting anything the hour has to be **validated**, which almost nobody does: most
people are carrying an hour they heard at home and never checked. Best to worst source: the
**hospital record**, which logs the minute; the **literal birth certificate** from the Civil
Register, which in Spain is free to request and carries the hour; the family book; something
written at the time. And last of all somebody's memory, which is what is nearly always used.

Two signs that an hour has been invented: **it ends on the hour or the half hour** — births do
not cluster there — and **two relatives give different hours**. When you ask, do not prompt:
saying "was it around three?" contaminates the answer.

If there is no way to validate it, you do not pick a pretty hour: you work with an interval and
you say so. **With a doubtful hour the planets in signs still hold; the houses do not.**

**Sidereal time.** The sidereal day is about 4 minutes shorter than the solar one, because while
the Earth turns it also advances along its orbit. Sidereal time says which degree of the zodiac
is culminating. You take it from the ephemeris and correct it for the hours elapsed and for the
**longitude** of the place.

**The angles.** With local sidereal time and **latitude** you enter the Tables of Houses and out
come the Ascendant and the Midheaven. Latitude is needed because the tilt of the horizon changes
with it — which is why at high latitudes some signs take hours to rise and others take minutes.

Then the planets are placed, interpolating their position for the exact hour, and the houses are
divided out. Today the software does it in a second. Doing it once by hand is what separates
someone who knows astrology from someone who knows how to use a program.

## Block 3 — The pieces (≈4 min)

Every position is a sentence in three parts:

| | Question | Role |
|---|---|---|
| **Planet** | which function? | the subject |
| **Sign** | in what manner? | the adverb |
| **House** | on what ground? | the place |
| **Degree** | with what precision? | it decides the aspects |

**Planets** = functions. Sun identity · Moon reaction and need · Mercury thinking and speaking ·
Venus valuing and joining · Mars wanting and pushing · Jupiter expanding · Saturn limiting and
structuring · Uranus breaking · Neptune dissolving · Pluto emptying and regenerating.

**Signs** = manners. There is no need to memorise twelve lists: every sign is a crossing of
**element** (fire burns, earth holds, air communicates, water feels) and **modality** (cardinal
sets off, fixed sustains, mutable adapts). Scorpio is fixed water: it feels and does not let go.

**Houses** = grounds, and they are learnt in opposite pairs: 1 me / 7 the other · 2 what is mine
/ 8 what is shared · 3 the near / 9 the far · 4 the root / 10 the public · 5 creating / 11 the
group · 6 the daily / 12 the hidden.

**The rulers** — this is what almost nobody teaches and what turns twelve compartments into a
system. Every house has a sign on its cusp, and that sign has a ruling planet. **Where that
planet sits connects the two houses.** House 2 ruled by Jupiter, and Jupiter in the 9th: money
arrives by way of study, abroad, or teaching. That is why an empty house is not mute: it is read
through its ruler. And most of them are empty, because there are ten planets for twelve houses.

**Aspects** = significant distances between planets. Conjunction 0° fuses · opposition 180°
confronts · trine 120° flows · square 90° costs and therefore builds · sextile 60° helps. A rule
worth more than any table: **the more exact, the more it rules.** A square at 1° weighs more than
a trine at 7°.

**Dignities** — not all planets weigh the same. In its own sign (domicile) a planet works
comfortably; in the opposite one (detriment) it works against the grain. And against the grain is
not misfortune: the effort makes a craftsman.

## Block 4 — How it is read (≈4 min)

Here is the craft, and it is what separates astrology from superstition.

**First you translate literally and clumsily.** "The Sun in Libra in the 2nd house" → *your
vitality expresses itself seeking agreement, on the ground of what you own.* Ugly on purpose: the
clumsiness stops you guessing.

**Then comes the real problem: the contradictions.** No aspect is read alone. Saturn squares
Venus — loving comes hard, there is reserve — but Jupiter trines it — there is warmth and
generosity. The beginner picks one side and throws the other away. That is the central error of
all badly done astrology.

The answer is not to choose. The person **has both**, and that is what has to be described:
someone warm who pulls back, generous and mistrustful at once. **A contradiction is not a failure
of reading: it is a portrait.** Real people are made of contradictions.

They are weighed by criteria, in this order: the **exactness** of the aspect, whether it is
**applying or separating**, the **dignity** of those involved, the **rulers** — which turn a
loose aspect into an argument with a subject and a ground — and the **general shape** of the
chart.

And above all of them, the master rule:

> **If three independent significators say the same thing, it is real.
> If only one says it, it is a murmur.**

**Last comes the synthesis.** The sentences are grouped by theme, the contradictions are settled,
and a running text is written. A chart is like a painting made zone by zone: each piece adds a
stroke, but a heap of strokes is not a painting. **It has to be squared up, and the one who does
that is the reader, not the chart.** Not everything goes in: a good reading uses perhaps a third
of the data.

## Block 5 — Going deeper, and where you stop (≈3 min)

A first reading produces blurred sentences: "there is tension with the father", "money is an
issue". True and useless.

Going deeper means turning them into precise sentences **by making more calculations, not by
adding adjectives**. The loop: general finding → which factors does it come from? → which
calculation sharpens it? → narrower finding → repeat.

The scale, one rung at a time: the ruler of the house and where it sits → the chain of dispositors
→ the aspects to the ruler by exactness → the derived house (reading somebody else's chart from
inside yours) → dignities → prediction.

A real example of the route: *"there is something with the father"* → the ruler of the 4th is in
the 12th, so **it is not conflict, it is absence** → Neptune trines it exactly, so it is an
idealised absence, not a harsh one → the derived house points to it coming to the father from his
own history. You stop there, because three instruments converge and the next calculation would
change nothing.

**And the stopping rules are worth as much as the loop.** You stop when three instruments
converge, when the next calculation would not change the advice, and above all **when you stop
calculating and start narrating**. The moment a new layer comes out of your head and not out of a
position, it is over. *"This is as far as the chart goes"* is a complete professional answer.

Two things to finish with. **Always separate data from interpretation**: "Venus is at 14°43′ of
Aries" is verifiable astronomy; "she loves impulsively" is arguable. And **distrust backwards
astrology**: knowing somebody's biography, anyone will find factors that fit, with any chart. The
only honest test is to deduce without knowing and check afterwards.
