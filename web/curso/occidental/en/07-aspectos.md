# Module 7 — Aspects

**Aim:** to know what an aspect is, how it is measured, and why exactness rules.

## The idea

An aspect is a **distance in degrees** between two planets that turns out to be significant.
Nothing more. It is measured along the ecliptic, in longitude.

| Aspect | Degrees | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| **Conjunction** | 0° | fuses. Neither good nor bad: it depends who |
| **Opposition** | 180° | confronts, strains, and forces you to look from outside |
| **Trine** | 120° | flows, makes easy — and is therefore wasted |
| **Square** | 90° | grates, costs, and therefore builds |
| **Sextile** | 60° | helps if you take it up |

Minor ones, which sharpen: semisquare (45°), sesquisquare (135°), quincunx (150°), semisextile
(30°).

## The orb

An aspect is almost never exact. The **orb** is the deviation allowed.

A rule worth more than any table: **the more exact, the more it rules.** A square at 1° weighs
more than a trine at 7°. The orb is not a permit, it is a measure of strength.

Working margins: 8° for conjunction and opposition, 7° for trine and square, 5° for sextile, 2-3°
for the minor ones. More when the Sun or Moon is involved.

## Applying and separating

If the faster planet is **moving towards** the exact aspect, it is **applying**: it presses, it
is under way, it still has to happen. If it has already passed it, the aspect is **separating**:
it is loosening, it has already operated.

It is a distinction almost nobody uses and it changes whole readings.

## Configurations

When three or more planets link up they form figures with a character of their own:

- **T-square** — an opposition with both ends squaring a third planet. The planet at the apex
  concentrates the whole strain and is the working point of the chart
- **Grand trine** — three planets at 120°. Ease and talent; also inertia
- **Yod** — two quincunxes and a sextile. Continual adjustment, a sense of uncomfortable fate
- **Stellium** — three or more planets together. One area of the chart carries the weight

## Questions

1. Which weighs more: Saturn squaring Venus with 0.5° of orb, or Jupiter trine Venus with 6°?
   Why?
2. Why can a trine be a problem?
3. A planet receives no aspects at all. Is it dead?
   *(No. It acts loose, negotiating with nobody. It usually shows a great deal, not a little.)*
4. What is the difference between an applying and a separating aspect?

## Common mistakes

- Treating the orb as a yes-or-no threshold instead of a scale
- Sorting aspects into good and bad
- Counting aspects between planets in signs that "ought" to form one, without measuring the
  degrees
- Forgetting aspects to the angles: a planet squaring the Ascendant counts
