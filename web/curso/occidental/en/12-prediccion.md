# Module 12 — Prediction

**Aim:** to know that the natal chart is a fixed map and that there are techniques for telling
**when** each part of it lights up. And to know what those techniques do not do.

## The idea

The natal chart never changes. What changes is which zone is switched on.

Three techniques, from slowest to fastest:

**Transits.** Where the planets are *today* in relation to the natal chart. The most direct and
the most used. The fast ones (Moon, Mercury, Venus, Mars) mark days and weeks; the slow ones
(Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune, Pluto) mark years, and they are the ones that matter.

A transit of Saturn over a natal point lasts months and usually passes three times — direct,
retrograde, direct — because the planet goes and comes back. Those three passes are three
moments of the same process.

**Secondary progressions.** One day after birth equals one year of life. At forty you look at the
sky of the fortieth day. It is slow, interior, and describes maturing rather than events. The
progressed Moon is the most useful indicator: it goes round the chart in about 27 years.

**Solar return.** The chart of the exact instant the Sun comes back to its natal degree each
year. It describes the tone of the year beginning.

## How they are used together

They are not read separately. You look for **coincidences**: a period matters when transit,
progression and solar return all point to the same thing. It is the convergence rule of module 9,
applied to time.

A transit on its own, unsupported, almost never produces anything visible.

## What activates what

Prediction does not invent: **it activates what is already in the natal chart**. A transit of
Jupiter over a difficult 7th house does not make it easy; it switches on its theme and offers an
occasion.

Which is why the order is always: the natal chart first, time afterwards. Whoever looks at
transits without knowing the chart is reading the clock without knowing which hour matters.

## What is not done

It will be said in full in module 13, but it is worth stating now:

- **Concrete events are not predicted.** The chart gives tendencies and periods, not facts.
- **No dates are put on death, serious illness or pregnancy.** Ever.
- **Misfortunes are not announced.** A hard transit is explained as a demanding period and what
  it asks for, not as a sentence.

## Questions

1. Why does a transit of Saturn pass three times over the same point?
2. Why does a transit of the Moon usually mean nothing?
3. A hard transit with nothing else pointing that way. What do you say?
4. Why do you have to read the natal chart before the transit?

## Exercise

On their chart: where Jupiter and Saturn are today in relation to it, and which houses they are
touching.

And an exercise in honest checking: have them look **backwards**. Have them locate an important
transit from years ago and check what happened then. It is the only way to calibrate without
self-deception — because the past can no longer be accommodated.

## Closing question

**What is the difference between saying "this is going to happen" and saying "this is being
activated"?**

## Common mistakes

- Looking at transits without having mastered the natal chart
- Reading an isolated transit and drawing conclusions
- Putting dates on events
- Forgetting that a slow transit has an entry, a middle and an exit, and that the middle is not
  the end
