# Module 14 — The other zodiac: jyotiṣa

**Aim:** to understand why the same birth gives different signs in India, and to know the system
that goes furthest in drilling down. This is an **informative** module: you do not learn jyotiṣa
here, you learn that it exists and what it offers.

## Why the signs change

The Western zodiac is **tropical**: it starts at the spring equinox, at the point where the Sun
crosses the celestial equator. It is a seasonal reference.

The Indian zodiac is **sidereal**: it starts from the fixed stars.

The two coincided about 1,700 years ago, but the Earth's axis wobbles — the precession of the
equinoxes — and they have drifted apart by a little over one degree every seventy years. Today
the difference, the **ayanāṁśa**, is around **24 degrees**.

Practical consequence: almost everybody has the Sun one sign earlier in jyotiṣa. Someone who is
Libra in Western astrology is usually Virgo in jyotiṣa.

**It is not that one of them is wrong.** They measure different things: one the relation to the
seasons, the other the relation to the stars. Arguing about which is "the true one" is arguing
about whether the metre is truer than the mile.

On top of that, jyotiṣa almost always uses **whole-sign houses**: house 1 is the whole sign of
the ascendant, house 2 the next one, and so on. No intermediate cusps.

`carta.py --sideral` calculates the sidereal version of the same chart, if they want to see it.

## What jyotiṣa has and Western astrology does not

Here is what matters in this module. The whole of module 11 — going down from the general to the
cause — rests on having instruments to go down with. **Western astrology has a scale of six
rungs. Jyotiṣa has dozens**, and that is why it reaches much deeper.

**Nakṣatras.** The zodiac divided not into 12 but into **27 lunar mansions** of 13°20′, each with
its planetary lord, its deity and its character, and each subdivided into four quarters. A planet
is not merely in a sign: it is in a nakṣatra, in a particular pada, and under the government of
that nakṣatra's lord — whose own condition then has to be examined. It is a whole rung of depth
that Western astrology does not have.

**Vargas.** Divisional charts: the chart is subdivided and produces **another complete chart**
for each matter. The D9 for marriage and the soul, the D10 for profession, the D12 for the
parents, the D7 for children. There are sixteen in common use. When something comes out blurred
in the main chart, you go to the varga of that matter and look at everything again from there.

**Daśās.** Systems of periods that say **when** each planet is activated, nested one inside
another: a major period of years, inside it a sub-period, inside that a sub-sub-period. You can
go down to the level of days. It is the answer to "when?" with a precision progressions do not
reach.

**Kārakas.** Significators, and of two kinds: the fixed ones (the Sun is always the father) and
the **variable** ones, assigned according to the exact degree each planet occupies in that
particular chart. It gives a second set of indicators for reading the same themes.

**Derived charts.** Taking another person's house as their ascendant and reading their whole
chart from inside yours.

## Causes inside causes

With those instruments the logic of module 11 can be taken much further. An example of the kind
of route it allows:

> Finding: *there is something with the mother.*
> → The lord of the mother's house, and where it sits.
> → The nakṣatra of that lord, and who governs it.
> → The condition of **that** governor, which is another planet with a history of its own.
> → The varga of the parents, where everything is rearranged.
> → The derived chart of the mother, taking her house as her own ascendant.
> → And inside that derived chart, **her** house of the mother — the grandmother.

At that point what was being looked for often appears: that what seemed to be a matter between
two people came from the generation before. **One cause inside another, brought on by a third.**

Jyotiṣa is said to have hundreds of thousands of calculable combinations. That is literally true,
and it is both its strength and its danger: with infinite instruments, **the stopping rules of
module 11 stop being a recommendation and become the only thing separating analysis from
invention.**

## What to take away from here

- Their Sun is probably one sign earlier in jyotiṣa, and that invalidates nothing
- Western astrology is stronger on psychology and on reading character
- Jyotiṣa is stronger on time and on the depth of causes
- **The discipline is the same in both**: count convergences, separate data from inference, and
  know when to stop

If one day they want to study it, let them study it as a whole system and not by mixing pieces.
Mixed systems do not work: each has its own internal logic and its own rules of weight.

invented?**
