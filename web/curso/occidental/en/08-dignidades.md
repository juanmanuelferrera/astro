# Module 8 — Dignities and retrogradation

**Aim:** to understand that not all the planets in a chart carry the same strength, and to be
able to measure which one rules.

## The idea

So far all the planets have looked equally weighty. They are not. A planet can sit in a sign
where it works comfortably or in one where it works against the grain. That is called
**essential dignity**.

| State | What it is | How it works |
|---|---|---|
| **Domicile** | in its own sign | at full strength, at home |
| **Exaltation** | in the sign where it is honoured | strong, somewhat excessive |
| **Detriment** | in the sign opposite its domicile | against the grain, with effort |
| **Fall** | opposite its exaltation | awkward, undervalued |

Domiciles: Sun in Leo · Moon in Cancer · Mercury in Gemini and Virgo · Venus in Taurus and Libra ·
Mars in Aries and Scorpio · Jupiter in Sagittarius and Pisces · Saturn in Capricorn and Aquarius.

Exaltations: Sun in Aries · Moon in Taurus · Mercury in Virgo · Venus in Pisces · Mars in
Capricorn · Jupiter in Cancer · Saturn in Libra.

**Important:** detriment and fall are not misfortunes. A planet against the grain produces
effort, and effort produces craft. A great many real capacities come from there.

## Accidental dignity

Apart from the sign, what counts is **where** it sits and **how** it arrives:

- **House**: angular weighs more than cadent
- **Aspects**: who speaks to it, and how exactly
- **Speed**: a slow planet weighs differently from a fast one
- **Nearness to the Sun**: a planet very close to the Sun is covered over

## Retrogradation

A retrograde planet is not going backwards: it **looks** that way from the Earth, because we
overtake it in orbit. It is an effect of perspective, not a physical event.

Interpretively: the function turns inward. It is revised, repeated, matures late, and is hard to
express outwardly. It is not a defect: it usually produces depth.

Mercury goes retrograde about three times a year, Venus every year and a half, Mars every two
years, and the outer planets nearly half the time. **Neptune being retrograde says almost
nothing about a person**: they share it with half a generation.

## The question that orders the chart

With this you can finally answer: **which planet rules here?**

Look at who accumulates most: ruler of the Ascendant, essential dignity, angular house, exact
aspects, part in configurations. That planet is the key to the chart, and it is where the
reading begins.

## Questions

1. Saturn in Capricorn in the 10th house, against Saturn in Cancer in the 12th. Which one rules?
2. Why can a planet in detriment end up as a capacity rather than a lack?
3. Why does Pluto retrograde say little about one particular person?
4. What happens to a planet half a degree from the Sun?

## Exercise

On their chart: the dignity of the ten planets and which of them are retrograde. Then the big
question: **which planet rules their chart, and why?** With arguments, not with hunches.

## Closing question

## Common mistakes

- Reading detriment and fall as bad news
- Forgetting accidental dignity: a planet in domicile but cadent and unaspected does little
- Reading the retrogradation of slow planets as though it were a personal trait
