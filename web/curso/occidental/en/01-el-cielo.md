# Module 1 — The sky from one point

**Aim:** to understand that a chart is a photograph of the sky *from one particular place*, and
to be able to point with a hand at where each thing was.

## The idea

An ephemeris says where the planets are today. That is the same for everybody: for the child
born in Madrid and the child born in Sydney the same minute, the Sun is at the same degree.

What turns "today's sky" into "the sky **from here**" is two circles.

### The horizon

The plane that separates what you see from what you do not. It is tangent to the Earth at the
point where you were born, and it stretches out to the sky. Everything above it was visible at
that moment; everything below it was under your feet, on the other side of the planet.

That plane cuts the ecliptic — the Sun's apparent path — at two points:
- in the **east**, the degree that was rising: the **Ascendant**
- in the **west**, the one that was setting: the **Descendant**

### The meridian

The circle that passes overhead and through the poles. It cuts the ecliptic at:
- the highest point, what was culminating: the **Midheaven**
- the lowest point, under your feet: the **Imum Coeli**

### This is why the hour and the place matter

The planets move slowly: the Sun takes a month to change sign. **The horizon moves one degree
every four minutes.** It goes all the way round every day.

That is the whole of it: the planetary positions you share with thousands of people born that
day. The horizon you do not. The horizon is the only thing that is yours.

### The four quadrants

The two circles cross at a right angle and cut the chart into four. Out of that comes a
sentence that orders half the course:

> Every house system agrees on the four angles. They differ only in how to subdivide what lies
> between them.

How each quadrant reads:
- **1st** (houses 1-3): below the horizon, in the east. The self forming, in private.
- **2nd** (4-6): still below, now turned towards the daily and the useful.
- **3rd** (7-9): emerging above the horizon, towards the other.
- **4th** (10-12): the public, the collective, what dissolves.

And the hemispheres: **above and below** the horizon is visible against hidden; **east and west**
of the meridian is what you determine against what is handed to you.

### Why east is on the left

Because the drawing is made facing south. With the south in front of you, the east falls to the
left. That is why the Ascendant is drawn on the left and the planets turn clockwise.

## Questions

1. If two children are born the same minute, one in Madrid and one in Tokyo, what is the same
   in their charts and what is different?
   *(Same: the planets in their signs and degrees. Different: the angles, and therefore the
   houses.)*

2. How far does the Ascendant move in twenty minutes?
   *(About 5°. One degree every four minutes, roughly, and depending on latitude.)*

3. A planet sits just below the Ascendant, a little under the horizon. Was it visible in the sky
   at birth or not? What is it about to do?
   *(It was not visible. It is about to rise in the east.)*

4. Why is the Midheaven not always the cusp of the 10th house?
   *(Because that depends on the house system. In equal houses they do not coincide. Keep this
   question: it is settled in module 6.)*

## Exercise

Open their chart. Have them locate the four angles and say, without help:
- how many planets were above the horizon at birth and how many below
- which quadrant carries the most weight
- which hemisphere is empty

And have them say it out loud as a sentence about a person, not as a piece of data.

## Closing question

**Why does the hour of birth matter more than the date?**

They should get there on their own: the date gives the planets, which they share with a lot of
people; the hour gives the horizon, which is the only thing that tells one chart from another
that day.

## Common mistakes

- Believing the Ascendant is "another sign of theirs". It is a point of the zodiac that was
  rising in the east.
- Confusing the Midheaven with the zenith. The zenith is directly overhead; the MC is where the
  meridian cuts the ecliptic.
- Thinking that up in the drawing is "north". Up is south, and up is what is visible.
