# The long way — a one-week plan

Seven days, about **three hours a day**, some 20 in all. At the end they can put up a chart by
hand and have written a complete reading of their own.

**How you use it:** when the long way begins, show them this whole plan so they know what they
are getting into. After that, every session starts by recalling **which day it is and what is due
today**.

Note the day in `progreso/alumno.md`.

---

## Day 1 — The sky and the hour · ~2 h
**Modules 1 and 2.**

The two circles that make a chart local, the four angles, quadrants and hemispheres. And then the
most fragile piece of data of all: the hour, with its four traps.

**Due:** their chart calculated and saved, with latitude, longitude and time zone confirmed by
them.

And an archive task that starts today and may take days: **trace their birth hour to a
document.** Not "my mother told me", but which piece of paper it comes from. The hospital record,
a literal birth certificate from the Civil Register, the family book, or something written at the
time. If it turns out to be family memory after all, let them know it and work with an interval.

The course can go on meanwhile with whatever hour they have — but let them know what they are
building on.

A deliberately gentle day. The next one is not.

---

## Day 2 — The calculation · ~3 h · **the hardest**
**Module 3.**

They put up their chart **by hand**. Interpolation, sidereal time, the longitude correction,
tables of houses.

**Warn them at the start:** today they will go wrong several times and that is normal. The first
chart by hand takes one to two hours.

**Due:** their chart put up by hand, with every step noted.

Your job today: make them do **every** calculation themselves. You check against `--pasos`, tell
them which step they went off at and by how much, and **you do not give them the right number**.
They redo it from there.

If they end the day without finishing, that is fine: have them finish it and bring it tomorrow.

---

## Day 3 — Angles and positions · ~2½ h
**Modules 4 and 5.**

The four angles as two axes, the ruler of the chart, and what rectifying really is. Then the
grammar: planet, sign, house, degree.

**Due:** the ten planets of their chart translated into literal, clumsy sentences. It will come
out ugly. It is raw material — on day 6 it turns into prose.

---

## Day 4 — Houses and aspects · ~3 h
**Modules 6 and 7.**

The twelve houses in opposite pairs, the systems of division, and **the rulers** — what turns
twelve loose compartments into a system.

**Two tables due:**
1. The twelve houses: which sign on the cusp, who rules it, where that ruler is lodged
2. Their aspects sorted by orb, calculated by them and only then compared with `carta.py`

A heavy day. If it has to be split, cut between the two tables.

---

## Day 5 — Dignities and contradictions · ~2½ h
**Modules 8 and 9.**

Which planet rules and why. And then the step up of the course: holding pieces that contradict
each other.

**Due:** the strongest contradiction in their chart, weighed with the five criteria and resolved
into **a single sentence** containing both halves. And a theme where three factors converge.

---

## Day 6 — The reading · ~3-4 h · **the longest**
**Module 10.**

They write the complete reading of their chart. Between 800 and 1500 words, running text, no
lists, no jargon.

They already have nearly all the material: the sentences from day 3, the tables from day 4, the
contradiction from day 5. Today it is ordered and written.

**Due:** the reading.

**Warn them of the trap:** it is their own chart and they will want to like it. Have them ask
themselves, sentence by sentence, whether they would write it the same way about a stranger.

---

## Day 7 — Going deeper, and closing · ~3 h
**Modules 11, 12, 13 and 14.**

Four modules, but only 11 carries heavy work.

- **11** — take the blurriest sentence of their reading and apply the loop to it. Document every
  layer and **where they stopped and why**
- **12** — where Jupiter and Saturn are today over their chart. And looking backwards: an old
  transit, checked against what actually happened
- **13** — reread their own reading marking what is data and what is inference, and which
  sentences they would not say out loud to the person. Usually the most revealing moment of the
  course
- **14** — informative, no exercise

**Final delivery:** the reading from day 6, revised with the criteria of day 7.

---

## Rules of the plan

**It is scaffolding, not a cage.** If a day runs long, the calendar moves. Never tell them they
are behind.

**Days 2 and 6 are a third of the effort.** Warn them from the start so they set time aside on
those two days and do not put them on their busiest one.

**If the pace does not fit**, offer them the same content over **two or three weeks**: one module
per session, two or three sessions a week. It is learnt as well or better.

**Every day starts the same way:** which day it is, what was due yesterday, what is due today.

**Every day ends the same way:** you update `progreso/alumno.md` with the day, the delivery and
the mistakes to go over.
