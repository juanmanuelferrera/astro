# Module 7 — The bhāvas

**Aim:** to understand whole-sign houses and, above all, **the house lords**, which is where
jyotiṣa does most of its work.

## Whole-sign houses

In classical jyotiṣa house 1 is **the entire sign of the Lagna**, from 0° to 30°. House 2 is the
whole of the next sign, and so on.

That has three consequences worth taking in at once:

**There are no intermediate cusps.** No house system to choose, no arguing about Placidus.

**The Lagna is not a cusp, it is a point inside house 1.** If the Lagna is at 23° of Karka, house
1 is still the whole of Karka, from 0° to 30°. The exact degree matters for other things — fine
aspects, the vargas — but it does not split the house.

**A graha is either in a house or it is not, with no ambiguity.** In Western astrology a planet at
29° of a sign can fall in one house or another depending on the system. Not here.

## The twelve bhāvas

| | Bhāva | What it deals with |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Tanu | body, temperament, life itself |
| 2 | Dhana | accumulated money, family, speech, food |
| 3 | Sahaja | younger siblings, courage, one's own effort |
| 4 | Bandhu | mother, home, land, happiness, education |
| 5 | Putra | children, intelligence, mantras, merit from before |
| 6 | Ripu | enemies, debts, illness, service |
| 7 | Yuvati | spouse, partners, the other |
| 8 | Randhra | longevity, crisis, the hidden, inheritance |
| 9 | Dharma | father, guru, fortune, pilgrimage |
| 10 | Karma | profession, action in the world, reputation |
| 11 | Labha | gains, elder siblings, desires fulfilled |
| 12 | Vyaya | loss, expenditure, retreat, mokṣa, abroad |

And three groupings that are used constantly:

**Kendras** (1, 4, 7, 10) — the pillars. A graha here carries a great deal of weight.
**Trikonas** (1, 5, 9) — the houses of fortune and dharma. The best of the chart.
**Duḥsthānas** (6, 8, 12) — the difficult houses. A graha here suffers, with exceptions.

## The house lords: the heart of the method

This is what to take away from the module.

Every bhāva has a sign, and that sign a lord. **Where that lord sits connects the two houses and
explains what the matter passes through.**

> The lord of the 2nd — money — is in the 9th. Money arrives by way of the 9th: study, abroad,
> teaching, the father, fortune.

That is no longer a label, it is an **argument with a subject and a ground**. And it is the normal
way of reasoning in jyotiṣa: nearly all the analysis consists of following house lords from one
place to another.

Questions are answered like this:

- Where does their money come from? → where the lord of the 2nd is
- What will their spouse be like? → where the lord of the 7th is, and what aspects it
- What is the relation between their home and their work? → lords of 4 and 10, and whether they
  are related
- And their mother? → lord of the 4th, plus the Moon as kāraka

**An empty house is not mute.** It is read through its lord. And most of them are empty: there are
nine grahas for twelve houses.

## Questions

1. Why is there no argument about house systems in jyotiṣa?
2. The lord of the 10th is in the 12th. What does that suggest about their profession?
3. What is the difference between a graha **in** the 7th and the **lord** of the 7th?
4. Why might a graha in the 6th not be bad news?
   *(Because in the duḥsthānas malefics sometimes work well: Saturn in the 6th defeats enemies and
   illness.)*

## Common mistakes

- Reading only the occupied houses
- Confusing "lord of the 7th" with "graha in the 7th"
- Bringing over from Western astrology the habit of splitting houses by degrees
