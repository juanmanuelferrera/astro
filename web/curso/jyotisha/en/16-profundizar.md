# Module 16 — Going deeper: from the general to the cause

**Aim:** the module this course exists for. This is where jyotiṣa goes further than any other
system — and where it is easiest to invent.

## The problem

A first reading produces sentences like "there is tension with the father", "money is an issue",
"she finds it hard to end relationships". They are true and they are useless: they do not say what,
nor why, nor what to do.

Almost everybody stops there and fills the gap with adjectives.

**Going deeper is the opposite: going back to the calculation.** Turning a blurred sentence into a
precise one by doing more arithmetic, not by adding words.

## The loop

1. **General finding** — state it as it came out
2. **Where does it come from?** — list the factors. Only one? It is a murmur; say so and stop
3. **Which calculation sharpens it?** — go up one rung of the scale
4. **A narrower finding** — rewrite the sentence
5. **Still blurred?** — back to step 2

## The scale in jyotiṣa

Western astrology has six rungs. Here there are many more, and that is the reason for studying it.
**One at a time**, not three:

1. **The lord of the bhāva** and where it is lodged
2. **Who looks at it** — dṛṣṭi of grahas and of lords
3. **Its dignity**, combustion, retrogradation, gaṇḍānta
4. **Its nakṣatra**, and the condition of the **lord of that nakṣatra** — a rung Western astrology
   does not have
5. **The varga of the matter**: D12 for the parents, D9 for the spouse, D10 for profession
6. **The kārakas**, fixed and variable, of the same matter
7. **The derived chart**: taking that person's bhāva as their Lagna and reading their chart from
   inside
8. **The daśā** that activates it, and when — has it happened already or is it still to come?

## A complete route

> **Layer 0.** "There is something with the mother."
>
> **Layer 1.** Where from? The lord of the 4th and the Moon, which is its kāraka. Two independent
> factors: go on.
>
> **Layer 2.** Where is the lord of the 4th? In the 12th. → *It is not conflict: it is absence,
> distance, or something unsaid.* The sentence has already changed.
>
> **Layer 3.** Who looks at it? Jupiter, with its fifth. → *An absence, but a protected one.*
>
> **Layer 4.** Its nakṣatra? Ruled by Saturn, and that Saturn is debilitated. → *The absence has
> weight and comes from far back.*
>
> **Layer 5.** The D12, which is the chart of the parents: everything is reread from there.
>
> **Layer 6.** The derived chart: the 4th house is taken as the mother's Lagna, and **her** 4th
> house is examined — the grandmother. → *That is usually where it appears that what looked like a
> matter between two people came from the generation before.*

One cause inside another, brought on by a third. That is what this system allows and why it is
worth learning.

## And here is the danger

With so many instruments, **you can go down indefinitely**. And past a certain layer you are no
longer calculating: you are narrating.

The stopping rules are worth as much as the loop. **They are not a recommendation: they are the
only thing separating analysis from invention.**

- **Stop when three independent instruments converge.** It is confirmed.
- **Stop when the next calculation would not change any advice.**
- **Stop when you stop calculating and start telling a story.** The moment a layer comes out of your
  head and not out of a position, it is over.
- **"This is as far as the chart goes" is a complete professional answer.**

And check that the testimonies are **genuinely independent**. If the lord of the 4th, the kāraka
and the MK turn out to be the same graha by three routes, that is **one** testimony said three
times.

## The discipline that holds it up

At every layer, separate them and say so out loud:

- **Data:** "Saturn is at 26°23′ of Dhanus, in the 6th house, retrograde." Astronomy. Verifiable.
- **Inference:** "That suggests a debt being gone over again." Interpretation. Arguable.

The deeper you go, the more inference looks like data. **It is not.** The precision of the
calculation does not carry any certainty over to the reading.

## Questions

1. "Money trouble" comes out. Which is the first instrument?
2. You have gone down seven layers and each sounds better. A good sign?
3. When are the lord, the kāraka and the MK **not** three testimonies?
4. What is the difference between going deeper and embellishing?

## Exercise

Take the blurriest sentence of their reading and apply the loop to it. Document **every layer**:
which instrument, what was found, how the sentence ended up.

And note **where they stopped and why**. That is worth as much as the result.

## Closing question

**If a system lets you go down twenty layers, what stops the last fifteen from being invented?**

## Common mistakes

- Confusing more layers with more truth
- Skipping rungs
- Never saying "I do not know"
- Presenting the inference of layer seven with the confidence of the data of layer zero
