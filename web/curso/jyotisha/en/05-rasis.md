# Module 5 — The rāśis and the sidereal zodiac

**Aim:** to understand why their Sun is one sign earlier than in Western astrology, and to know
that this does not mean one of the two is wrong.

## The two zodiacs

The Western zodiac is **tropical**: it starts at the spring equinox, the point where the Sun
crosses the celestial equator. It is a **seasonal** reference.

The Indian zodiac is **sidereal**: it starts from the fixed stars. It is a **stellar** reference.

The two coincided about 1,700 years ago. But the Earth's axis wobbles — the precession of the
equinoxes — and they have drifted apart by a little over one degree every seventy years.

That separation is called the **ayanāṁśa**, and today it is worth about **24 degrees**.

> sidereal longitude = tropical longitude − ayanāṁśa

Since each sign measures 30°, those 24° are enough to move most people into another sign. Someone
who is Libra in Western astrology is usually Kanyā — Virgo — in jyotiṣa.

**It is not that one is right and the other wrong.** They measure different things: one the
relation to the seasons, the other the relation to the stars. Arguing about which is the true one
is arguing about whether the metre is truer than the mile.

Several ayanāṁśas are in use. By far the most widespread is the **Lahiri** or Citrapakṣa, which is
what this program uses and what the government of India employs.

## The twelve rāśis

| | Rāśi | Element | Quality | Lord |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Meṣa | fire | movable | Mars |
| 2 | Vṛṣabha | earth | fixed | Venus |
| 3 | Mithuna | air | dual | Mercury |
| 4 | Karka | water | movable | Moon |
| 5 | Siṁha | fire | fixed | Sun |
| 6 | Kanyā | earth | dual | Mercury |
| 7 | Tulā | air | movable | Venus |
| 8 | Vṛścika | water | fixed | Mars |
| 9 | Dhanus | fire | dual | Jupiter |
| 10 | Makara | earth | movable | Saturn |
| 11 | Kumbha | air | fixed | Saturn |
| 12 | Mīna | water | dual | Jupiter |

The elements and qualities work exactly as in Western astrology. **Movable, fixed and dual** are
the equivalents of cardinal, fixed and mutable.

## What does change: the lord carries more weight

In Western astrology the sign describes a character. In jyotiṣa the sign matters above all
because **it has a lord**, and whatever that lord does in the chart drags the whole sign and the
house it occupies along with it.

Which is why in jyotiṣa there is less talk of "he is very Scorpio" and more of "the lord of the
fourth is in the ninth". The sign is a postal address; the lord is who lives there.

   *(5° − 24° = 341° of the zodiac, that is 11° of Kanyā. It changes sign.)*
2. Why does the ayanāṁśa grow with time?
3. Does someone with the Sun at 27° of a tropical sign change rāśi?
   *(No: 27 − 24 = 3°, it stays in the same sign. Only those below 24° change.)*

## Common mistakes

- Believing jyotiṣa "corrects" Western astrology, or the other way round
- Subtracting a fixed 24°: the ayanāṁśa changes over the years and has to be calculated for the
  date
- Reading the rāśi as character and forgetting its lord
