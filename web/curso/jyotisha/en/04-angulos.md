# Module 4 — The four angles

**Aim:** to understand that the angles are four and form two axes, and to know what to do when
the hour is not reliable.

## The idea

Module 1 produced four points. Now they get a name and a meaning.

**Ascendant (AC).** The degree that was rising in the east. The body, the temperament, the way
you show up in the world, the first gesture. It is not "a second sign of yours": it is a point.

**Descendant (DC).** Directly opposite. The other: partner, associates, opponents. What you are
not, and therefore either seek or collide with.

**Midheaven (MC).** What was culminating. Vocation, public life, what is seen of you from far
off, reputation.

**Imum Coeli (IC).** Under your feet. Origin, home, family, root, the place you withdraw to.

**They are two axes, not four loose points.** You cannot read the AC without the DC, nor the MC
without the IC. Whoever has the AC in one sign has the DC in the opposite one by definition:
what you are and what you look for are two halves of the same thing.

## The ruler of the Ascendant

The planet that rules the sign of the AC is called the **ruler of the chart**. Where that planet
sits — which sign, which house, which aspects — says where a whole life is heading.

It is one of the first things to look at and one of the most informative.

## Rectification

When the hour is doubtful it can be narrowed. This is not magic: it is a procedure, and it has
rules.

**How it is done properly:**
1. Fix the possible interval. "In the afternoon" will not do; it has to be bounded.
2. See what changes inside that interval. Does the sign of the AC change? Do planets change
   house? If nothing changes, there is nothing to rectify.
3. Take **dated, verifiable events** from the life: moves, operations, deaths, marriages,
   changes of work. The more the better.
4. See which hour makes the predictive techniques line up with those dates.
5. Check it against build and temperament — but **at the end, as confirmation**, never as the
   main argument.

**How it is done badly, which is the usual way:** pick the Ascendant that "fits" the person and
then list the traits that confirm it. That is not rectifying, it is decorating a decision
already made. Knowing what someone is like, you will always find traits that fit any sign.

**The honest rule:** a rectification holds up if it predicts something you did not know. If it
only explains what you already knew, it is worth nothing.

## Questions

1. If the AC is in Scorpio, where is the DC, and what does that say?
2. Why do the MC and IC not depend on latitude while the AC does?
   *(The meridian cuts the ecliptic the same way at any longitude; the horizon tilts with
   latitude.)*
3. Someone has the AC in Libra and Venus in the 12th house. What does that tell you about the
   direction of their life?
4. When is rectification NOT needed?
   *(When nothing relevant changes inside the margin of doubt.)*

## Exercise

On their own chart: the four angles, the ruler of the Ascendant, where it is lodged, and one
sentence that takes AC and DC together as a single axis.

And a sensitivity test: have them recalculate the chart half an hour earlier and half an hour
later, and see what moves. That teaches them how much weight their own birth hour can carry.

## Closing question

**What is the difference between rectifying and choosing the ascendant we like best?**

## Common mistakes

- Reading the AC as if it were a second Sun
- Forgetting the DC. Half the chart is lost
- Rectifying backwards from the biography and believing something has been proved
