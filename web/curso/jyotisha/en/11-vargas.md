# Module 11 — The vargas

**Aim:** to understand the instrument Western astrology does not have: **a different complete chart
for each matter**.

## The idea

A rāśi measures 30°. Divide it into nine stretches of 3°20′ and assign a sign to each stretch, and
you get **another entire chart** — the navāṁśa or D9.

This can be done with any divisor. Each division produces a new chart, and each one **answers a
different matter**.

| Varga | Division | What it speaks of |
|---|---|---|
| **D1** rāśi | 1 | life as it is lived |
| **D2** horā | 2 | wealth and livelihood |
| **D3** drekkāṇa | 3 | siblings, courage, effort |
| **D7** saptāṁśa | 7 | children and descendants |
| **D9** navāṁśa | 9 | the soul, the spouse, real strength |
| **D10** daśāṁśa | 10 | profession and public karma |
| **D12** dvādaśāṁśa | 12 | the parents |
| **D16** ṣoḍaśāṁśa | 16 | vehicles, comfort, pleasures |
| **D30** triṁśāṁśa | 30 | evils and misfortunes |
| **D60** ṣaṣṭyāṁśa | 60 | karma accumulated from before |

There are sixteen in common use. These ten cover almost everything.

## What they are really for

Here is the important part, and it links to module 16.

**When a reading of the D1 comes out blurred, you go to the varga of that matter and look at
everything again from there.**

"There is something with the parents" in the D1 is a useless sentence. You open the **D12**, which
is the chart of the parents, and read it whole from inside: what is in its 4th house, where its
lord is, who looks at it. An answer comes out that the D1 could not give.

It is the equivalent of changing lens, not of adding a detail.

## The navāṁśa rules

Of them all, the **D9** is the most important after the D1, for two reasons.

**It is the chart of the soul and of marriage.** It is read for the spouse and for inner dharma.

**And it measures the real strength of the grahas.** A graha that looks strong in the D1 but falls
badly in the D9 does not deliver what it promises. And the other way round.

Hence the idea of **vargottama**: a graha that occupies **the same sign in the D1 and in the D9**.
That strengthens it a great deal — it is a graha consistent with itself on both planes.

## The rule that avoids looking foolish

A varga **is not read on its own**. The D10 does not say what your profession will be by itself: it
shades and sharpens what the D1 already says. Whoever draws conclusions from a divisional without
having read the main chart is doing ornamental astrology.

And the high vargas — D30, D60 — are **extremely sensitive to the hour**. One minute of error moves
grahas into another sign in the D60. If the hour is not validated as module 2 requires, those
charts cannot be used.

## Questions

1. How wide is one stretch of the navāṁśa?
2. "Something with the father" comes out in the D1. Which varga do you open, and why?
3. What is a vargottama graha?
4. Why does the D60 demand more precision of hour than the D9?

## Exercise

On their chart: look at the **D9** and compare each graha with the D1. Which improve, which get
worse, which are vargottama?

Then open the **D10** and see whether it says the same thing as the 10th house of the D1 or shades
it.

## Closing question

**Why is a varga never read without the D1 in front of you?**

## Common mistakes

- Reading a divisional as though it were an independent chart
- Using D30 or D60 with an approximate hour
- Forgetting to check for vargottamas
- Believing that more vargas means more truth
