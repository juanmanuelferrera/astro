# Module 15 — The yogas

**Aim:** to understand what a yoga is, to recognise the main ones, and — above all — not to collect
them.

## What a yoga is

A **combination** of factors that produces a recognisable result. It is not an aspect nor a loose
position: it is a configuration with a name.

There are literally thousands catalogued in the texts. Most popular manuals devote themselves to
listing them, and that is exactly the error this module wants to avoid.

## The ones actually used

**Rāja-yoga** — the most quoted. It forms when the lord of a **kendra** (1, 4, 7, 10) and the lord
of a **trikona** (1, 5, 9) are related: by conjunction, by exchange of signs, or by mutual gaze. It
indicates rise, authority, recognition.

**Dhana-yoga** — combinations that produce wealth, mostly among the lords of the 2nd, 5th, 9th and
11th.

**Gaja-kesari** — Jupiter in a kendra **from the Moon** (not from the Lagna: that is the usual
mistake). Intelligence, good name, protection.

**Nīca-bhaṅga rāja-yoga** — the one from module 10: a debilitation cancelled and turned into
strength.

**Pañca-mahāpuruṣa** — five yogas, one for each non-luminary graha, when it is **exalted or in its
own sign** and also **in a kendra** from the Lagna:

| Yoga | Graha |
|---|---|
| Ruchaka | Mars |
| Bhadra | Mercury |
| Haṁsa | Jupiter |
| Mālavya | Venus |
| Śaśa | Saturn |

**Kemadruma** — the Moon with no grahas in the adjoining houses and none conjunct. It is read as
mental solitude, though it is easily cancelled and is almost never as grave as it sounds.

## The warning that matters more than the list

**A yoga is not a promise.** Three things condition it:

**It has to be activated.** A rāja-yoga formed by the lords of the 9th and the 10th does nothing
until the daśā of one of those two grahas arrives. It may never arrive in a lifetime.

**It can be broken.** If the grahas involved are debilitated, combust or in a duḥsthāna, the yoga
exists on paper and yields nothing.

**Almost every chart has several.** With nine grahas and twelve houses, one always turns up.
Somebody finding you three rāja-yogas means nothing in itself — the question is whether they are
intact and whether their daśā arrives.

Manuals that promise greatness because "you have Haṁsa yoga" are selling smoke. The honest reading
is: which yoga is there, what state its grahas are in, and when it is activated.

## Questions

1. Where is Gaja-kesari counted from, the Lagna or the Moon?
2. What is needed for a rāja-yoga to produce anything?
3. Why does finding several yogas in a chart say very little?
4. What is a pañca-mahāpuruṣa, and what two conditions does it ask for?

## Exercise

On their chart: look for any pañca-mahāpuruṣa — a non-luminary graha, exalted or in its own sign,
in a kendra — and for any nīca-bhaṅga.

For each yoga they find: are the grahas involved sound? When does their daśā arrive? Has it already
passed?

## Closing question

**Why is a yoga with no daśā to activate it only a signed paper nobody has cashed?**

## Common mistakes

- Collecting yogas the way one collects stickers
- Counting Gaja-kesari from the Lagna
- Announcing greatness from a yoga without looking at the state of its grahas
- Frightening people with a Kemadruma that is almost always cancelled
