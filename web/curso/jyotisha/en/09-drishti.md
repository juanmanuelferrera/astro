# Module 9 — Dṛṣṭi: the gaze

**Aim:** to understand that the Vedic aspect **is not measured in degrees**, and to know who looks
at whom in a chart.

## The difference from Western astrology

In Western astrology an aspect is an angular distance with its orb: Venus at 91° from Saturn is a
square with 1° of orb.

Not in jyotiṣa. **Dṛṣṭi goes by whole sign.** A graha looks at a complete house, and it makes no
difference which degree it stands on. There are no orbs to calculate and no tables of exactness.

The word means "gaze", and that is the image: a graha *looks* towards certain houses, and what it
looks at is affected by its nature.

## Who looks where

**Every graha looks at the seventh house from where it stands.** That is universal: if you are in
house 3, you look at the 9th.

And three have **special gazes**, on top of the seventh:

| Graha | Also looks at the… |
|---|---|
| **Mars** | 4th and 8th |
| **Jupiter** | 5th and 9th |
| **Saturn** | 3rd and 10th |

Rāhu and Ketu are usually given the 5th, 7th and 9th, like Jupiter, though not every school
agrees.

## How it is counted

Always **including the house you start from**. A graha in house 2 that looks "at the seventh" looks
at house 8: count 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8.

It is the number one source of errors at the start. Count on your fingers until it comes by itself.

## What it means to be looked at

A dṛṣṭi **transmits the nature of the one looking**. Jupiter's gaze protects and expands what it
touches; Saturn's delays it, hardens it and makes it last; Mars's stirs it up and cuts it.

And there are no "good" and "bad" aspects by shape, as in Western astrology, but by **who is
looking**. There is no square and no trine: there is Saturn looking at you or Jupiter looking at
you.

Jupiter's gaze on a house is considered one of the best protections there is. It is said to be
enough to save an afflicted house.

## Dṛṣṭi between lords

The next rung, and the one that really pays: when the **lord** of one house looks at another house,
it is connecting the two matters.

> The lord of the 7th looks at the 2nd → what concerns the spouse reaches money and family.

It is the same reasoning as module 7 with the lords, but by way of the gaze instead of by way of
lodging. The two are read together.

## Questions

2. Saturn in house 1 — which houses does it look at?
   *(The 3rd, the 7th and the 10th, counting from the 1st.)*
3. Why are there no orbs in jyotiṣa?
4. What is the difference between Jupiter being **in** your 7th house and **looking at** it?

**which house receives the most gazes** and which receives none.

And a reading: does Jupiter look at any difficult house? If so, there is a protection there.

## Common mistakes

- Counting without including the house you start from
- Bringing over from Western astrology the idea of aspects being good or bad by shape
- Forgetting that lords also look, not only the grahas in themselves
- Giving Mars Saturn's aspects or the other way round: the 4th and 8th are Mars's, the 3rd and
  10th Saturn's
