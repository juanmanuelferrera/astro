# Module 6 — The nakṣatras

**Aim:** to understand the division jyotiṣa has and Western astrology does not, and why it is
finer than the signs.

## The idea

Besides the twelve rāśis of 30°, the zodiac is divided into **27 nakṣatras** of 13°20′ each.

This is not a decorative layer: **it is older than the signs** in the Indian tradition, and for
many purposes it carries more weight. The word means "lunar mansion", and that is its origin: the
Moon travels through one a day, so the 27 nakṣatras are the lunar cycle spread around the sky.

Each nakṣatra is itself divided into four **padas** of 3°20′. And each pada points to a sign of
the navāṁśa, which is the bridge between this division and the vargas of module 11.

## The three pieces of each nakṣatra

**The name**, which carries its symbol and its character — Aśvinī the horseman, Revatī the one who
nourishes.

**The deity**, which gives its function. Pūṣan in Revatī is the one who guides souls in transit;
Yama in Bharaṇī is the one who governs limits and passages.

**The lord**, which is what gets used most. The 27 nakṣatras are shared out in cycles of nine
lords, always in the same order:

> Ketu · Venus · Sun · Moon · Mars · Rāhu · Jupiter · Saturn · Mercury

That order repeats three times and covers the 27.

## What the lord is for

Two things, and both of them matter.

**One: it is the clock.** The lord of the nakṣatra the Moon occupies at birth determines the daśā
a life starts in. The whole vimśottarī system of module 12 hangs from that.

**Two: it is a rung of depth.** A graha is not merely in a sign: it is in a nakṣatra, and that
nakṣatra has a lord **whose own condition then has to be examined**. If the lord of the fourth is
in a nakṣatra ruled by Saturn, and that Saturn is debilitated, that information travels down into
the fourth house.

Western astrology has nothing equivalent. It is a whole rung of analysis that exists only here.

## The Lagna has a nakṣatra too

And it usually says more about temperament than the rāśi does. Someone with the Lagna in Karka but
in the nakṣatra Āśleṣā — ruled by Mercury — will have a mental quickness that the sign Karka on
its own does not explain.

## Questions

1. How wide is a nakṣatra, and how wide is a pada?
2. Why are they called "lunar mansions"?
3. What does the Moon's nakṣatra at birth determine?
4. A graha sits in a nakṣatra whose lord is debilitated. What do you do with that information?

## Exercise

On their chart: the nakṣatra and pada of each graha, and of the Lagna. Then, for the three most
important grahas, **look at where the lord of their nakṣatra is** and what condition it is in.

That is where the depth module 11 talked about begins to show.

## Common mistakes

- Treating them as folkloric ornament
- Stopping at the nakṣatra's name and never looking at its lord
- Forgetting the pada, which is what links to the navāṁśa
